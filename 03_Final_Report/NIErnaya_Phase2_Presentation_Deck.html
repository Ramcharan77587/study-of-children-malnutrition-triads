Rationale & Design Decisions

  • Socioeconomic Vulnerability Index captures the gap between harmful social norms (child marriage) and women's empowerment (literacy) using z-score normalization for comparable scaling
  • Sanitation–Water Gap quantifies WASH infrastructure imbalance — districts where water coverage far exceeds sanitation suggest open defecation persists despite piped water access
  • Maternal Care Gap identifies districts where women deliver in facilities but miss the antenatal care window — a service quality indicator
  • Nutritional Burden Index is quarantined from modeling pipelines (data leakage guard) but used for descriptive district ranking and geographic profiling

Preprocessing Pipeline Summary

  • Missing values: No imputation needed (0% missingness across all analytical columns)
  • Outlier handling: IQR-flagged values retained — verified as authentic survey data from extreme-burden districts
  • Type coercion: All percentage strings converted to float64; Census codes parsed to nullable integers
  • Placeholder removal: "Data Not Available" dummy district excluded (J&K administrative gap)
  • VIF screening: All predictor VIFs in [1.19, 2.50] — no harmful multicollinearity

Key Insights & Conclusions
Evidence-Backed Policy Implications
11

Five Evidence-Based Insights

1 — Sanitation Is the Strongest Single Lever

Improved sanitation shows the strongest negative correlation with underweight (r = −0.583) and stunting (r = −0.503). Districts with <50% sanitation coverage average 39.8% stunting vs. 29.1% in districts with >80% coverage. This aligns with the environmental enteropathy pathway — open defecation causes subclinical gut inflammation that blocks nutrient absorption.

2 — Female Literacy Is Equally Decisive

Female literacy (r = −0.545 with underweight, r = −0.539 with stunting) operates through improved care-seeking behavior, dietary diversity, and delayed marriage. Districts where literacy exceeds 85% show median stunting of 27.3% — nearly 10 percentage points below the national mean.

3 — Early Marriage Is the Strongest Risk Factor

Child marriage (before 18) shows the strongest positive correlation with underweight (+0.481) and stunting (+0.428). Adolescent mothers are themselves nutritionally depleted, leading to intergenerational transmission of growth deficits.

4 — The Stunting–Wasting Duality Demands Distinct Strategies

Bihar and UP dominate stunting (chronic, developmental); Gujarat and Maharashtra dominate wasting (acute, emergency). Treating them interchangeably in policy is a resource misallocation error.

5 — Water Access ≠ Water Safety

Near-universal "improved water source" coverage (median 97%) produces negligible correlation with malnutrition (r = −0.037). The indicator measures infrastructure type, not biological water safety or faecal contamination at the point of use.

Correlation ≠ Causation — all findings are associational NIErnaya Phase 2
